Teaching philosophy

Language teaching should empower learners.

I want students to become confident, autonomous, and lifelong learners who can use English to pursue their goals and participate more fully in their communities.

Support for growth

Learning another language takes patience, courage, and consistent practice. Students benefit from intellectual challenge and emotional support. Vocabulary is central to communication, so I teach learners strategies for noticing, remembering, retrieving, and applying new words. I also work to normalize mistakes and encourage risk-taking so learners can build resilience as their skills develop.

Communication with purposeful support

My teaching is informed by communicative language pedagogy. I plan lessons with clear objectives and real-world relevance, then scaffold the practice learners need to participate. Modeling, guided practice, sentence frames, and purposeful partner work can help students move toward more independent communication. I want students to do much of the talking, negotiating meaning, and applying language themselves.

Responsive assessment and reflection

Formal and informal assessment help me understand what students can do and what support they need next. I observe learners during activities, respond to their questions, and reflect after lessons so I can adjust future instruction. I aim to integrate reading, writing, listening, speaking, and vocabulary whenever it supports the learning goals.

Relationships and learner agency

When I taught English in Thailand, I worked with learners across ages, professions, and proficiency levels. Games, group work, and individual support helped more hesitant students participate. One learner later shared that the encouragement in class helped them overcome a longstanding fear of speaking English in front of others. That experience reinforced my belief that relationships and thoughtful lesson design both matter.

I try to model diligence, patience, and courage, and to help learners notice their own growth. I want my classroom to be a place where students feel valued, capable, and supported as they take on challenging work.
